From brine to crystal

How natural solar evaporation transforms seawater (35 g/L) and sub-soil desert brine into >99.4% pure food-grade sodium chloride through precise fractional crystallization.

Watch the chemistry change

Drag the slider to observe how mineral density dictates what crystallizes out at each density threshold:

NaCl Crystallizer Pan (Peak Purity)
Pure Sodium Chloride (NaCl) crystallizes rapidly! Dunaliella salina microalgae tint the water vivid pink.
Prime Food-Grade Salt
01

Intake & Sluice Gates

3.0° – 5.0° Baumé

High-tide seawater or deep desert sub-soil brine (drawn from 40–80 ft aquifers) enters massive primary settling reservoirs. Suspended silt and organic sediments settle naturally by gravity over 7–10 days.

02

Carbonate Precipitation

6.0° – 12.0° Baumé

Under relentless tropical solar heat, water evaporates. Insoluble ferric oxide (Fe₂O₃) and calcium carbonate (CaCO₃ / chalk) precipitate out on the pan floors, preventing cloudiness in downstream pans.

03

Gypsum Evaporators

13.0° – 24.5° Baumé

Brine volume contracts by over 75%. Calcium sulfate dihydrate (Gypsum: CaSO₄·2H₂O) crystallizes out in massive crusts. Trapping gypsum here is essential to avoid hard, gritty, insoluble impurities in the final salt.

04

Dunaliella Bloom & Crystallization

25.5° – 29.5° Baumé

The magical crystallization window! Super-saturated brine triggers the rapid growth of halophilic microalgae Dunaliella salina and archaea, producing high beta-carotene that protects crystals and gives pans their iconic pink glow. Pure cubic halite (NaCl) forms glistening sheets.

05

Bittern Liquor Discharge

29.5° – 32.0° Baumé

Crucial quality & worker safety step: Once brine hits 29.5° Bé, the remaining "mother liquor" (Bittern) must be swiftly drained off. If left, bitter magnesium chloride (MgCl₂) and magnesium sulfate (MgSO₄) deposit, causing bitter taste and caustic chemical burns.

06

Raking & Pyramidal Heaping

Harvesting Phase

Salt workers rake the crystalline beds with traditional wooden panjhas or mechanical harvesters. Crystals are gathered into distinct triangular heaps (kumbhas) along the dykes, allowing residual mother liquor to drain out naturally under the sun.

07

Washing, Iodization & Packing

Refining & Food Safety

Harvested crystals are washed with saturated clean brine to strip surface impurities, centrifuged to remove moisture, dosed with Potassium Iodate (KIO₃ at 30 ppm as mandated by national iodine deficiency prevention laws), and sealed in moisture-proof packaging.

Inside the crystal

The geometry of pure salt

Sodium chloride naturally nucleates into perfect face-centered cubic (FCC) crystal lattices. In slow, controlled solar evaporation pans, the halite crystals form majestic hollow hopper cubes and gleaming transparent geometric blocks.

The people who make our salt

This is a student-led humanitarian project built from public reporting, field context, and cited research. It is designed to make the realities of salt workers visible and to support better welfare and safer work conditions for communities such as the Agariyas, Uppalam workers, and Mithgaudas.

Livelihoods at the heart of the landscape

“The Agariya’s body does not decompose easily when cremated; their legs, soaked for decades in hyper-saline brine, contain so much salt that they must be gathered separately and buried with care.” This haunting reality describes the sacrifice behind every pinch of salt consumed across the country.

Explore the six pillars

Working conditions that need attention

Caustic Brine Ulcers

Constant daily contact with 26° Baumé brine causes intense epidermal keratosis, deep fissures, and non-healing salt ulcers that prevent workers from walking without excruciating pain.

Blinding UV Glare

White salt flats reflect up to 90% of intense solar ultraviolet radiation. Without UV-400 polarized eyewear, workers suffer chronic photokeratitis, pterygium, and premature cataract blindness before age 45.

Heat Stroke & Kidney Stones

Summer temperatures in the desert flats frequently exceed 46°C–48°C. Combined with heavy physical raking and acute shortages of potable drinking water, over 60% of workers suffer from nephrolithiasis and chronic kidney distress.

The Diesel Debt Trap

Pumping sub-soil brine requires diesel generators that consume 1,200 to 1,800 liters of diesel per family per season, eating up to 65% to 70% of total salt revenues and leaving families bound to loan sharks (vyaparis).

Seasonal Child Disconnection

Migrating for 8 months every year to remote mudflats forces entire families into makeshift huts (kumbhas) with no electricity, no sanitation, and no access to local village schools, causing intergenerational poverty.

Six ways to support better livelihoods

Solar Submersible Brine Pumps

Replacing noisy, polluting diesel generators with solar photovoltaic pumping arrays. This immediately eliminates diesel fuel bills, saving each salt family ₹75,000 to ₹1,00,000 per season and preventing over 2.5 tonnes of carbon emissions per pan.

Direct Financial Impact: +60% household seasonal net income.

Ergonomic Podiatric & Dermal Gear

Standard heavy gumboots trap sweat and cause fungal infections. We advocate for custom-designed lightweight, breathable, white neoprene-lined salt boots with non-slip treads, accompanied by barrier petroleum-lanolin salves to completely prevent salt fissures.

Health Impact: 95% reduction in chronic salt ulcers and foot lesions.

UV-400 Polarized Safety Eyewear

Distributing wrap-around, shatter-resistant polarized sunglasses engineered specifically for 90% albedo salt environments. Side-shields prevent peripheral dust and glare intrusion, stopping ultraviolet photokeratitis in its tracks.

Ocular Impact: Preserves natural vision and halts early cataract onset.

Solar Desalination & Potable Water Hubs

Installing localized solar-powered reverse osmosis (RO) drinking water kiosks and insulated water transport tankers that deliver 20 liters of cold, mineralized potable water directly to worker shacks across remote rann expanses.

Welfare Impact: Complete elimination of dehydration-induced kidney failure.

Mobile 'Rann Shalas' & Tele-Clinics

Deploying mobile tent classrooms (Rann Shalas) equipped with digital learning tablets and solar power to ensure children never drop out during the 8-month salt cycle, alongside specialized Sanjeevani medical vans providing dermatological and podiatric care.

Generational Impact: Zero school dropouts and continuous child education.

Direct Fair-Trade Producer Cooperatives

Organizing salt farmers into democratic producer cooperatives with direct institutional tie-ups to public distribution systems and FMCG companies. Eliminating exploitative middlemen guarantees a transparent minimum floor price for raw crystal salt.

Economic Impact: Triples the per-ton price received by grassroots producers.

Better science. Better salt.

How optimizing the chemical evaporation sequence simultaneously protects workers from hazardous bittern and produces medical-grade sodium chloride, gourmet artisanal salts, and valuable industrial minerals.

Production quality

Optimized Purity Protocol

  • Automated Baumé Cutoff Sensors: Continuous digital density sensors alert operators the exact moment brine hits 29.5° Bé, ensuring bittern is drained automatically so workers never have to wade into hazardous caustic mother liquor.
  • Microplastic Multi-Stage Filtration: Intake seawater passes through natural bio-sand filters and micro-mesh screens before reaching crystallizers, guaranteeing 100% microplastic-free table salt for human consumption.
  • Enclosed Solar Tunnel Drying: Transitioning from open ground drying to enclosed solar tunnel dryers prevents airborne windblown dust, road soot, and bird droppings from contaminating crystalline salt.
  • Codex & FSSAI Conformance: Guarantees >99.4% NaCl purity on dry basis, water insoluble matter <0.1%, and exact 30 ppm Potassium Iodate fortification for cognitive health.
Circular economy

Valorization of Bittern By-Products

  • Magnesium Hydroxide & Epsom Salts: Treating drained bittern with lime yields Magnesium Hydroxide Mg(OH)₂ used in pharmaceuticals (antacids) and flame retardants, converting hazardous waste into high-value revenue.
  • Bromine Extraction: India’s salt bitterns are rich in bromine ions (up to 2,500 ppm), supplying vital raw materials for agrochemicals, pharmaceuticals, and energy storage batteries.
  • Potash (SOP) Fertilizer: Recovering potassium salts from mixed salt bittern fractions provides organic Sulphate of Potash (K₂SO₄) for Indian farmers, reducing reliance on expensive imported fertilizers.
  • Artisanal Gourmet Salt Branding: Marketing delicate surface crystals as "Fleur de Sel" and large desert cubic crystals as "Kutch Baragara Salt" commands 10x the price of generic bulk salt, funding community worker welfare trusts.
